## Content Document Summary

The DOCX provides the authoritative content direction for the future website. It presents Green for Life Rwanda (GLR) as a community-based Rwandan non-profit organization focused on environmental protection, sustainable agroforestry, and livelihood improvement for small-scale farmers, women, youth, and vulnerable communities.

### Home

The homepage should welcome visitors to the official Green for Life Rwanda website and explain that GLR works with communities to restore ecosystems, improve food security, and build resilient livelihoods through sustainable agroforestry practices across Rwanda.

Recommended homepage emphasis:

- Community-based environmental action.
- Sustainable agroforestry.
- Smallholder farmer support.
- Ecosystem restoration.
- Food security and resilient livelihoods.
- Strong calls to explore programs, projects, impact, and ways to support GLR.

### Who We Are

GLR is a community-based non-profit committed to environmental protection and sustainable livelihood improvement for vulnerable populations. It works closely with smallholder farmers, women, and youth through practical local solutions addressing climate change, food insecurity, and poverty.

The document also notes that GLR encourages farmers to work together to raise seedlings and plant trees that bind soil, retain rainwater, and provide food and firewood, with small financial incentives for their efforts. GLR is based in Gisagara District, Southern Province, and intends to expand its activities nationwide.

### Mission

Green for Life Rwanda's mission is to protect the environment and improve the livelihoods of small-scale farmers by promoting sustainable agroforestry practices in communities across Rwanda.

### Vision

The vision is a world where people and the environment coexist in harmony without compromising one another.

### Core Values

The document identifies these value themes:

- Inclusive participation by all people and communities.
- Community participation as part of good local governance and development.
- Commitment to marginalized communities and meaningful improvement in their lives.
- Partnership as a key organizing principle, including alliances and linkages with aligned institutions and individuals.

### History

GLR was founded in 2017 and legally registered in 2021. It was established by students from the former Higher Institute of Agriculture and Animal Husbandry (ISAE Busogo), specializing in agroforestry.

The founding motivation came from rural smallholder farmers' challenges, including drying streams, declining food security, and longer distances to collect firewood for fuel and fencing.

### What We Do

The main program areas are:

- Agroforestry Promotion
- Climate Action
- Youth Environmental Education
- Community Livelihoods

The content should move away from generic wording and toward these four clear program pillars.

### Agroforestry Promotion

GLR promotes agroforestry as a practical, community-driven, impact-oriented solution for small-scale farmers. The major activities are:

- Community-based tree nursery development.
- Tree seedling distribution and planting.
- Small livestock support for soil fertility.
- Training in agroforestry and good agricultural practices.

The expected result is that communities can grow more, earn more, and protect the environment.

### Climate Action

Climate action is presented as central to GLR's work. Activities include:

- Climate-smart agroforestry.
- Community tree planting and landscape restoration.
- Sustainable agriculture practices such as composting, organic pest control, mulching, and water conservation.
- Youth engagement in climate leadership.
- Women empowerment for climate resilience.
- Environmental awareness and advocacy.

The climate vision is resilient communities that protect natural resources, adapt to climate change, and contribute to a greener planet.

### Youth Environmental Education

GLR works with school environmental clubs to help youth become environmental champions. Activities include:

- Training youth on environmental protection.
- Supporting schools to establish and maintain fruit tree orchards and vegetable gardens.
- Training and mentoring teachers as environmental club leaders.

### Community Livelihoods

GLR supports farmer environmental clubs with practical skills for environmental protection and income generation. Key interventions include:

- Village Savings and Loan Associations methodology.
- Business skills and entrepreneurship.
- Vegetable gardens for income generation and household nutrition.
- Good agricultural practices, including organic pesticides and organic fertilizers.

### Projects

The content document identifies three main projects for the future site:

1. Agroforestry Project for Mushonyi Community-Led Restoration in Rutsiro District

   This project targets Mushonyi and Boneza sectors of Rutsiro District. It aims to restore 200 hectares of degraded land using sustainable land management and eight selected agroforestry tree species. It targets 357 smallholder farmers through training, seedling distribution, and technical support.

2. Enabling Environmental Clubs in Sustainable Agroforestry in Nyanza Sector, Gisagara District

   This project strengthens environmental clubs in Nyanza Sector. It trains 360 environmental club members in tree nursery management, agroforestry, and climate-smart agriculture. It includes one community tree nursery, 42 agroforestry species, and 180 small livestock for selected members.

3. School Greening and Environmental Education Project, Nyanza Sector, Gisagara District

   This project empowers 600 youth from environmental clubs in 5 schools. It focuses on fruit tree planting, school vegetable gardens, sustainable agroforestry, climate action, nutrition, and environmental stewardship.

### Impact

Use these final impact numbers:

- 350 smallholder farmers trained in agroforestry and environmental protection.
- 329,425 trees planted across Gisagara and Bugesera Districts.
- 365 hectares of degraded land restored in Gisagara and Rutsiro Districts.

Note: the DOCX says "365 hectares of degraded land restored in two districts"; the approved instruction clarifies the districts as Gisagara and Rutsiro.
